## Corrections

The previous overviews repeatedly presented access to a permission, role, service, or vulnerability as a guarantee of Domain Admin or SYSTEM authority. This pass replaces those guarantees with documented distinctions between identity, authorization, object data, and administrative scope. It also corrects specific conceptual problems:

- LAPS manages local administrator passwords and can manage DSRM passwords; gMSA provides a managed domain service identity across multiple servers. These are different account-management mechanisms.
- An NTLM challenge response is distinct from the password and its underlying hash. Kerberos tickets, surrounding replies, and session keys also have different roles. The comparisons use protocol descriptions and their security assumptions; they do not assess acquisition or guessing procedures.
- DACLs govern discretionary permissions, while SACLs identify access attempts to audit. AdminSDHolder/SDProp permission matching is distinct from parent-object inheritance, which is disabled for protected accounts and groups.
- Enterprise CA templates express issuance policy. CA identity attestation, template permissions, certificate use, and revocation status have separate responsibilities. The blanket characterization of every listed certificate issue as arbitrary-user authentication was removed.
- SMB file access, WMI management data, DCOM remote objects, WinRM management sessions, and remote desktops are different concepts. A protocol name does not establish a resulting service account or administrative token.
- A trust supports cross-domain authentication; it does not grant uniform resource permissions. Operator groups and endpoint-management roles also have different responsibilities and scopes.
- An RODC's Password Replication Policy controls password caching. The assumption that every controller necessarily holds every account password was removed.
- CVE records, CVSS severity, and CISA KEV observations serve different purposes. No specific vulnerability, exploit prerequisite, or affected installation was assessed.

The conclusions that a protocol, category, diagram, or role title alone does not establish an operational outcome are editorial inferences from the documented models. They are not results from testing a domain or an attack sequence. The table below identifies the sources for these corrections; exact claims and source sections are recorded in each node.
